# Published human+LLM outcome ratios against model-alone and expert+model ceilings (R_cap, R_hum)

Generated 2026-09-08 by agent PR1. JSON twin: `results/published_ratios.json`.

This table reports, for published controlled studies that include a human+LLM arm, the ratio R of that arm's score to a ceiling measured in the same study on the same items. Two ceilings are kept separate because they answer different questions. R_cap divides the human+LLM score by a model-alone score produced by the study team's own prompt or scaffold; it asks how much of the capability the study team could elicit from the model was realised by participants, and it can exceed 1 when interaction adds value beyond a zero-shot run. R_hum divides the human+LLM score by an expert+model arm; it asks how far ordinary users fall short of skilled users of the same model, and it confounds domain knowledge with elicitation skill unless the experts differ from participants only in model-use skill. Both are reported where defined. Neither is a measurement of the five EST behaviours; they are outcome-level ratios only. Every number below is backed by a verbatim quotation or by released primary data, listed under 'Verbatim sources'. Quotations are reproduced as printed except that Unicode minus signs, en dashes in numeric ranges, multiplication signs and plus-minus signs are normalised to ASCII (-, x, +/-); one source em dash inside a Zhang 2026 quotation is left as printed.

## Reading the table

Medical reasoning trials dominate the computable rows (Goh 2024, Goh 2025, McDuff 2025, Everett 2026, Qazi 2026, Bean 2026, Brodeur 2024), and in the physician trials with a same-model, study-team-prompted ceiling R_cap sits between 0.83 and 0.98 (Goh 2024 0.826 on medians; Qazi 0.861; McDuff 0.875; Everett 0.943 to 0.977; Goh 2025 0.984), meaning physicians with the model recovered most but not all of what the study team's prompt obtained from the same model on the same cases. The lay-public trial (Bean 2026) is the outlier at or below 0.364 for condition identification, and the paper attributes much of the gap to users not conveying scenario information to the model. Only Zhang 2026 (in silico dual-use biology) and Shao 2024 (CTF) sit near the CB or cyber uplift setting; there R_cap ranges from about 0.46 to 0.70 against the best standalone model (Shao 0.458; Zhang MBCT 0.514, LAB-Bench 0.698) but reaches or exceeds 1 against the mean standalone model on HPCT (1.005) and on HLE against both mean and best (2.598, 1.318), so the sign of the human contribution depends on the benchmark and on whether the ceiling is the average or the post hoc best model. R_hum is available only from the OpenAI 2024 released data (students+GPT-4 over experts+GPT-4, 0.770), and it mixes domain expertise with model-use skill and, per PR1v, model variant (experts had a research-only GPT-4 without refusals), so no published study yields a clean R_hum. None of these studies measured the five EST behaviours or any other process measure of elicitation skill, so every R here is an outcome-level ratio that bounds the room left for all human-side factors together (elicitation skill is one of several).
