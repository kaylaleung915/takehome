#### Reading the j_verify association
The association of judged verification (j_verify) with familiarity (rho 0.249, p=0.0021; with frequency rho 0.137) is specific to this subsample and is suggestive rather than established. It rests on 50 'Somewhat familiar' and 50 'Very familiar' judged persons, of whom 47 have at least one verify-coded conversation (64 such conversations), against 12 of 50 'Not familiar' persons (17 conversations); the estimate is not fragile to single persons (V12b: jackknife range 0.24 to 0.27, dropping the 10 most influential persons leaves p < 0.03, person-permutation p = 0.002, any-verify 12/20/27 of 50 by level with Fisher Not vs Very p = 0.004), but it is heterogeneous by draw and confounded with conversation length: it is carried by the 63 persons retained from the first run (rho 0.438) and absent in the 87 newly judged persons (rho 0.109, p = 0.32; level means 0.133 / 0.081 / 0.102), it is flat across familiarity in 2-4-turn conversations (83% of the data) and appears only in conversations with 5 or more user turns (verify rate 0.118 / 0.220 / 0.323 by level), and partialling out turns and words per turn reduces it to 0.154 (p = 0.06). Familiar persons in this subsample hold longer conversations (see the representativeness table), and longer conversations give more opportunity to verify. The deterministic Layer-1 analogue (challenge lexicon) gives rho 0.011 [-0.047, 0.064] with familiarity on the full sample of 1283 persons and rho 0.049 within the judged persons. The judge has no human validation labels on PRISM, and its verify code agrees with the Layer-1 challenge lexicon at conversation-level kappa 0.250 (section 5). The result is one of 10 measures x 2 predictors tested without multiplicity correction.

## 7. What this does and does not show
- Does: in N>1,200 real people each holding 6 short conversations with 2023-24 chat models, whether a Not/Somewhat/Very familiarity self-rating and a 6-level frequency-of-use item track observable differences in how they prompt (length, constraints, continuation, challenge/verification, judged specificity), and how rank-stable those behaviours are within person over one collection window. The self-report items are close analogues of the 'prior LLM experience' items used as covariates/proxies in uplift trials (OpenAI 2024; AISI), so a weak association here is evidence against treating such items as a proxy for elicitation behaviour, at least in open-ended chat.
- Does not: (1) PRISM conversations are opinion / values / controversy chat plus unguided small tasks, not technical information extraction against a target list, so coverage, uptake and persistence-after-refusal are unmeasured and verification is near floor; generalisation to bio/cyber uplift settings is an assumption. (2) Conversations are short (median 2-3 user turns, ~10-15-word prompts) and the platform forced >=2 turns, compressing behavioural variance. (3) Models are 2023-24 systems and all 6 conversations were collected in one window (most within an hour), so Q2 is short-run consistency, not stability across days, learning, or model generations. (4) The Layer-2 judge is unvalidated on PRISM (no human labels here; its self-written targets make 'specificity' partly judge-defined); Layer-1 regexes are crude (named-entity and challenge heuristics have visible false positives/negatives) and English-centric. (5) Familiarity is heavily imbalanced (Not familiar ~10%), frequency level 0 conflates 'never used' with 'item not shown', and both are single self-report items from the same session. (6) Multiple measures x two predictors are tested without multiplicity correction; read effect sizes and CIs, not p-values. (7) Person-level means over 6 conversations still carry topic variance; ICC(1,k) indicates how much averaging helps.
- Further limits (added after independent verification, V12): (8) Single-conversation reliability of the binary Layer-1 features is low (ICC(1) 0.06 to 0.24 across spec_open, continued_3plus, challenge, narrow; section 4), so most conversation-level variance in these features is within-person noise or topic variance, which caps the variance that any person-level predictor could explain and attenuates the Q1 associations toward zero. (9) Frequency of use is not a clean direct-use gradient: 77 of the 1074 respondents at frequency levels 1-5 answered No or Unsure to the direct-use item, and level 0 (n=209, of whom 209 answered No/Unsure) is a null frequency value rather than a 'never' response. (10) Part of the within-person stability of n_followups and continued_3plus reflects a person-level habit of stopping at the platform minimum of 2 user turns (34% of conversations stop at exactly 2), which is behaviour but not necessarily elicitation skill.
- Layer 2 specifically: the judged persons (n=150; per level {'Not familiar at all': 50, 'Somewhat familiar': 50, 'Very familiar': 50}) are a subsample, and the representativeness table in section 3 shows whether deterministic features behave the same way inside it as in the full sample. The j_verify association with familiarity (rho 0.249) is subsample-specific and suggestive: it rests on 47 familiar persons with any verify-coded conversation, the full-sample deterministic analogue (challenge) is rho 0.011 [-0.047, 0.064], the judge is unvalidated against human labels on PRISM, and its verify code agrees with the Layer-1 lexicon at kappa 0.25. V12b adds that it is carried by the 63 first-run persons (rho 0.438) and absent in the 87 newly drawn (rho 0.109), and that it is confounded with conversation length (partial rho 0.154, p = 0.06). It should not be reported as offsetting the Layer-1 null without that context. Verdict of the representativeness check (V12b): the judged subsample over-represents long-conversation 'Very familiar' persons relative to a random balanced draw (95th percentile), so Layer-2 familiarity gradients are partly a subsample artefact; Layer-1 full-sample results are the ones to cite.

## 8. Known gaps
- Judge (Layer 2) has no human validation labels on PRISM; V2 labels were on synthetic EST transcripts only.
- Layer-1 named-entity (M2) and challenge regexes are heuristics with unquantified precision/recall on this corpus; kappa vs the judge is the only cross-check.
- Participants with !=6 conversations (about 100) excluded from Q1/Q2; survey-only participants excluded.
- No multiplicity correction across 6+4 measures x 2 predictors.
- Within-type split-half uses single conversations per half (2 per type), so it is a lower bound relative to pooled 3-vs-3.
- lm_frequency_use level 0 merges survey nulls (no direct use / not shown); results also reported for familiarity which has no nulls.
- Assistant turns passed to the judge are the participant-chosen reply only (of 4 at turn 0, of 2 later); unchosen replies the participant also read are not shown to the judge.
- Layer-2 subsample: the first run judged 63 persons (40/9/14 by familiarity) selected by the chance intersection of two seeded draws (V12 issue 1); the code now takes a nested level-balanced cut that retains those persons and fills each level from the seeded draw order. Layer-2 associations are subsample-specific until the representativeness table in section 3 shows the subsample tracks the full sample.
- data/prism/judge_cache holds one orphan file from a smoke run (hash 3c6a03cdefb3bfef, c1/user1) that is outside the sample; prism.py drives from the sample, not the directory, so it is unused. Anyone loading the cache wholesale should drop it.
- OLS delta-R2 bootstrap intervals are bounded below by zero by construction and are not significance tests.
