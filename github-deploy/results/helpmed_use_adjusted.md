## What changed since the previous pass (V25 and V24b)

- The control arm is not a no-model arm. Controls were told to "use any assistance they would typically use at home (for example, internet search)". In the post-task survey 48.8% of controls rate an LLM at least slightly influential on an answer and 30.9% fairly or very influential; in free text 29 of 600 control sessions name an LLM product (ChatGPT 17, Gemini 8, Copilot 4, Claude 1) and 16 more mention AI answers in general, 15 of them Google's AI overview (the previous pass reported the combined 45 as product mentions; V25b corrected it). Non-compliance is two-sided. Every run now passes `--control-access usual_sources` with those two exposure shares, and the tool labels every contrast "assigned platform model versus usual sources" and lists "effect of model use against no model" under not identified. Point estimates are unchanged; their meaning is narrower than the previous pass stated.
- Self-reported use: the previous pass counted 122 non-users by requiring a "not at all influential" rating on both scenarios, which silently treated the 196 one-scenario participants' missing second rating as influence. The rule is now "not at all influential on every scenario the person completed": 139 non-users, pi_c 0.861, CACE -0.163 (was -0.160).
- Covariates: the ten first-scenario indicators are added (the scenario drawn first is a randomised design variable, arm balance chi-square p 0.14). An intermediate run that added "drew scenario X in any slot" and `n_scenarios` produced a 7.2% narrowing (AUC 0.64) on the turns run; that was leakage. The LLM arm has 194 of 997 single-scenario participants against 2 of 301 controls, so `n_scenarios` and the any-slot indicators are arm-dependent and post-treatment. Only the first draw is used; the 7% vanished. `n_scenarios` stays in the file without the `pre_` prefix.
- Estimator: the covariate-tightened bounds are now the median over 30 random fold assignments, recomputed under 5 fresh seeds; "beyond noise" requires the original and every re-seed to clear the permutation null's 95th percentile. The md prints the narrowing in outcome units and control SD, not only as a percentage.
- After V25b: the verdict has four outcomes (beyond noise; at the edge, some re-seeds clear; clears every seed but cross-fit AUC not above 0.5; within noise) plus "not tested" when `perm_reps` is 0, and a magnitude block labels any real narrowing under 5% of the width "negligible" (a reporting floor, stated in the output, not a test). The 45 "control sessions naming an LLM" figure was wrong: it is 29 naming a product plus 16 naming only generic AI. The first-scenario covariate is clearly pre-treatment only for two-scenario participants (arm balance against controls: two-scenario p 0.37, one-scenario p 0.0072), so every run was repeated on the two-scenario subset (`data/helpmed/trial_real_twoscen/`); that subset conditions on a post-treatment event and is a sensitivity check, not a cleaner estimand. The claim that the first-scenario indicators supply most of the detectable signal is withdrawn (below).
- Simulation: calibrated to the trial's own control outcome distribution (Gaussian copula) and realised pi_H, so widths are on the 0 to 1 outcome scale. The generic "rho 0.7 gives 28%" line from the previous pass is replaced by the table below.
- Exposure line: mean user turns per participant is 4.6 (the previous pass printed 4.5); the md also prints how many treated participants sit at zero.
- A rendering bug in the intensity line (an f-string prefix dropped during the V25 edit, printing `{_f(q[0.1], 1)}` verbatim) was found in this pass's output, fixed in `est/estimands.py`, re-rendered into all ten affected md files from their JSON, and guarded by test M22 (mutation caught).

Reading the two tables together: with the first-scenario indicators the botsurfaced narrowing is 1.7% on the full sample and 2.4% on the subset; without them 0.26% and 2.0%. So the indicators add 1.4 points where their pre-treatment status is doubtful and 0.4 points where it is clear, and the survey items alone do more on the subset (2.0%) than on the full sample (0.26%). The 0.4-point contribution sits inside the survey-only run's own re-seed spread on the subset (0.8% to 2.9%), so the subset cannot establish that the indicators add anything at all (V27). None of the twelve runs reaches the 5% floor. The subset's larger survey-only narrowing is not evidence the covariates work better for two-scenario people; the subset is selected on a post-treatment event and the permutation null's p95 is essentially unchanged (0.14% against 0.16%), so a 1.7-point difference between two sub-5% numbers is within what fold noise and sample change produce (re-seed ranges 0.8 to 2.9% on the subset). The turns run's null p95 is 3.3% on the subset against 0.5% on the full sample, which is the three-cell estimator's known behaviour when pi_H drops (0.36 here); that run is within noise either way.

## Reading against the established problem (section 7)

1. Per-arm use distribution: delivered as a distribution. No non-use in the access sense (the platform forced a message), so CACE = ITT under that definition. Use is shallow: median 4 turns across one or two scenarios, one in twenty sent 10 or more. The control arm's exposure is also now on the card: about half of controls say an LLM influenced an answer, so the ITT is platform model versus usual sources including other LLMs, and the "effect against no model" is listed as not identified. Confidence high; arithmetic on the authors' own scoring and survey.

2. Complier or principal-stratum estimand with a baseline covariate: delivered as a set, and the set is wide ([-0.35, +0.32] on a 0 to 1 outcome for the stratum in which the model surfaced the answer). Twenty-three real pre-randomisation columns (six LLM-use-frequency items and their mean, education, age, sex, English, health-search habit, medical practice, and which of the ten scenarios was drawn first) predict that stratum at cross-fit AUC 0.53 to 0.60 on the full sample and 0.52 to 0.58 on the two-scenario subset. In four of six full-sample runs and three of six two-scenario runs the narrowing clears the permutation null in the original and all five re-seeds, so at n 1,298 the covariates carry detectable information about who gets a relevant answer out of the model. The size is what matters: at most 0.011 outcome units (0.03 control SD) off a width of 0.67 on the full sample, 0.024 units (0.06 control SD) on the GPT-4o two-scenario subset; the tool labels all of it negligible (under 5% of the width). An earlier version of this paragraph said the first-scenario indicators supply most of the detectable signal (full sample: AUC 0.525 without them, 0.578 with; 0.26% against 1.7%). V25b pointed out that the indicator is only clearly pre-treatment for two-scenario participants, and on that subset the indicators add 0.4 points to a 2.0% survey-only narrowing. The claim is withdrawn: what the data support is that neither the survey items nor the scenario indicators narrow the set by more than a few percent, and which of the two contributes more depends on a sample restriction that is itself post-treatment. Where the scenario indicators do contribute, the signal is about which scenario the model finds easy (bot-surfaced rate by scenario 45% to 86%), not about the person. Confidence high that self-report and demographics are practically worthless for this estimand on this trial; moderate on the exact AUCs (three cells, ridge LPM); low on any ordering of the two covariate groups. The calibrated table sets the bar an EST-type pre-test has to clear at this n: AUC about 0.83 for a 35% narrowing, 0.94 for 59%. Nothing here shows the EST clears it.

3. Ceiling arm: reported from the paper's direct-prompting baseline rather than an arm in the data release. The number that matters for the project's premise: the best-served half of the treated arm reached a 0.456 identification rate against a 0.949 model-alone rate on the same rubric. Even among people the model told the answer, half the answers did not make it to the submission. That 0.49 gap is an upper bound on what better extraction and uptake could have bought, and it is larger than any effect the trial could detect. Confidence high for the direction; the ceiling is typed from the paper (not recomputed) and is a model-alone, full-information ceiling, not an expert-paired one.

Negative result kept: the naive "stratum H minus all controls" number changes sign across H definitions (-0.014, +0.042, -0.104, -0.103, +0.024) while every set straddles zero. A card that reported the naive number would report noise with a sign. Second negative result kept: the post-treatment covariate set (any-slot scenario indicators plus `n_scenarios`) produced a narrowing seven times the largest legitimate one; a pipeline that accepts any column prefixed `pre_` will manufacture such results, which is why the ingest docstring now states the rule.

## Gaps

- No judge run on the 1,800 transcripts (about 1,800 calls; not authorised). A coded extraction score would give a genuine top-third stratum defined by what the user asked rather than what the model said.
- H strata are post-treatment and about half the arm; bounds on a binary-ish outcome near the logical limits; the covariate-tightened width is structurally limited when pi_H is near 0.5.
- Stratum H in the botsurfaced runs is defined by the model's behaviour as much as the user's; it is not a "motivated user" stratum and the docs no longer call it one.
- Control-arm LLM exposure is self-reported (influence ratings and free text); the 48.8% / 30.9% shares are an upper and lower reading of the same survey item, not a measured use rate.
- "First scenario is pre-treatment" assumes the scenario was assigned before the participant could act on it and was not chosen by the participant; the release orders sessions by `generated_datetime` but does not document the assignment mechanism. The pooled arm balance (chi-square p 0.14) hides an imbalance for the 194 one-scenario LLM participants (p 0.0072 against controls), for whom the paper's account (API failures on GPT-4o and Llama 3 with impacted participants replaced; a stopping rule changed to 600 interactions per arm; Methods archived in `data/helpmed/raw/paper_text/`) does not say which scenario was assigned first and does not cover the 28 one-scenario Command R+ participants. The two-scenario subset is balanced (p 0.37), which is consistent with random assignment and does not prove it.
- The two-scenario subset restricts on `n_scenarios`, a post-treatment event concentrated in the GPT-4o and Llama arms; its rows are a sensitivity check on the covariate claim, not a cleaner estimand.
- One run per model arm was done only for GPT-4o.
- Ceiling values typed from the paper's text, not recomputed from `data/baselines/` on GitHub (not copied).
- The authors' fuzzy-match scoring taken as given; no re-scoring check against `gptextracted_clean_examples.csv`.
- Bootstrap B = 1000 per run; the outer interval on set endpoints is conservative for the parameter inside the set, not an Imbens-Manski confidence set for the set.
- Authors not contacted; nothing here has been seen by them.
- V25b confirmed the ingest and the full-sample numbers and refuted the 45 free-text count and the covariate ordering claim (both corrected here). V27 (`verification/V27_v25b_followup.md`) then reproduced the two-scenario filter, the balance p-values, all twelve table rows and both results tables against the JSONs; it found one tool defect (the md estimate table labelled the two "at the edge" subset runs "NOISE-LEVEL narrowing" while the verdict paragraph said "AT THE EDGE"; fixed in `est/estimands.py` and rerun, STATE.md 12:58) and four wording errors in `data/helpmed/README.md` (corrected there). The two-scenario rows are no longer single-sourced.
